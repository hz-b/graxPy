Solver

Takagi-Taupin computes the crystal Bragg reflection of the strained lattice; a coating then only attenuates the beam. RCWA and Nevière compute the grating at grazing incidence: the fingers, the surface displacement and the coating, with every diffraction order recorded on its own as an ideal line detector would. {% if solver == "rcwa" %}This grating has a coating, so RCWA is preselected.{% else %}This grating has no coating, so Takagi-Taupin is preselected.{% endif %}

The x and z resolutions sample one period of the structure for the solver, as on the grating pages; layer and finger interfaces are always slice boundaries, and the z resolution sets how finely the surface ripple and planarized interfaces are sliced. Each angle and amplitude is one solve, run in parallel by the workers ("auto" or a number).

Beam

Beam sizes are FWHM of a Gaussian centred on the electrodes; the part outside the electrode area reflects as the undeformed crystal. The angular resolution combines the beam divergence and the monochromator bandwidth.

It follows the photon energy (for an energy scan, the middle of the energy range) until you edit the angles.

Run opens a page with the progress, the map of angle against energy and the rocking curve of each energy, filled in as they are computed, all at the one deformation amplitude (0.012 nm per volt). With Takagi-Taupin the angle is measured from the Bragg angle of each energy and each curve is normalized to the undeformed crystal at that energy.

Run opens a page with the progress, the map of angle against amplitude and all rocking curves, filled in as each amplitude is computed. The amplitude is the zero-to-peak surface-normal displacement of the lattice below the fingers. Give the amplitudes as a list or as a linear range. The defaults, 0.012 nm per volt from 0 to 10 V (0 to 0.12 nm in eleven steps), reproduce Fig. 4 of Vadilonga et al., Opt. Express 29, 34962 (2021).
